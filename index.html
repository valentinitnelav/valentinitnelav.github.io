About

I build tools that help ecologists see more of nature with less effort.

From 2016 to 2025 I worked as a scientist at the Helmholtz Centre for Environmental Research (UFZ) and the German Centre for Integrative Biodiversity Research (iDiv), on plant–pollinator networks, statistics and R packages for ecological analysis. My PhD (Martin Luther University Halle-Wittenberg, 2026) combined computer vision, machine learning and affordable smartphones to monitor pollinators automatically. Since July 2026 I have continued this work at UFZ and iDiv as a postdoc.

I care about open science, careful data work and software that is pleasant to use. I work mostly in R and Python (statistics, GIS, deep learning with YOLO object detectors) and build Android apps in Flutter/Dart and Java. I also like calm cats, calm people and soothing colours.

I am open to research and data science roles in academia and industry. The quickest way to reach me is through LinkedIn.

Current work

Smartphones are cheap, robust and everywhere. I turn them into field cameras that record and recognise insects and other animals, so that monitoring biodiversity does not depend on expensive equipment or many hours of manual image sorting. Both apps are open source.

FaunaPulse

An AI wildlife camera on your phone

FaunaPulse turns an Android smartphone into a camera that detects, tracks and identifies animals in real time. The AI runs on the phone itself, offline, and you can load your own detection and classification models. Its first scientific use is measuring visitation rates: how often, and for how long, pollinators visit a flower.

  • Real-time detection and tracking on the device, no cloud needed
  • Bring your own models, with Python tools to convert them
  • Motion trigger, time-lapse with night mode, or scheduled video

FaunaLapse

A second life for old phones as time-lapse cameras

FaunaLapse, the sister app of FaunaPulse, turns old or inexpensive Android phones into time-lapse cameras that watch one spot, such as a flower, for visiting insects. It takes short photo bursts or video clips with the screen off, keeps only the square you choose, and rests the camera between bursts to save battery and keep the phone cool. It needs no AI, so it runs on phones from as far back as 2017.

  • Screen-off photo bursts or short video clips
  • Crops to the area you choose, for smaller files
  • Tested on Android 8 to 16; field tests are planned
